# Aethergard — provisional setting notes

These are **design hypotheses**, not fixed story canon. Keep the story and world generator in dialogue without hard-coding every narrative beat into geography.

- An earlier civilization combined magic with practical engineering.
- A catastrophe may have destroyed or scattered many magic practitioners, leaving everyday communities to rebuild without much of that knowledge.
- Some ancient roads, crossings, and structures remain usable even by people who don't know how they work.
- Magic is uncommon and may be feared; different communities disagree about why the catastrophe happened.
- Contemporary towns should feel inhabited: workplaces, noncombat NPCs, quests, agriculture, trade, family life, and routine travel.
- Wilderness may be dangerous, but ordinary people, animals, settlements, and peaceful encounters should occupy meaningful parts of the map.
- The story should permit multiple traversal methods rather than a single optimized path. A roadless fishing village may be reached by raft; a mountain outpost by a pass, climbing gear, or surviving old infrastructure.

## World-generation implications

1. Keep modern roads and ancient infrastructure as distinct route types.
2. Put human settlements near water, resources, trade corridors, or defensible positions, but allow story-driven exceptions.
3. Give a future settlement blueprint work sites, homes, public spaces and NPC roles—not just a marker on a map.
4. Preserve multiple routes to story objectives and validate that no generated map makes the story impossible.
5. Reserve space in world saves for quest anchors, NPC spawns, wildlife and regional economies; don't implement those systems prematurely.
